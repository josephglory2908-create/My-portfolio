01 / Dashboard preview

Compare demand and cancellation patterns.

The report’s overview page compares booking volume, average daily rate, market segments, and hotel performance. A second page explores cancellations by lead time, market segment, deposit type, and arrival month.

02 / Business question

How does booking behavior vary across hotels and customer segments?

The analysis compares City Hotel and Resort Hotel to show when booking demand rises, which segments account for the most bookings, and how cancellation rates vary with booking lead time. These comparisons can help frame further investigation into reservation planning and cancellation management.

03 / Data preparation

Build a consistent foundation for reporting.

  1. Preserve the source records

    Retained all 119,390 rows. Without a booking ID, identical rows were not assumed to be duplicate reservations.

  2. Prepare analysis fields

    Created arrival dates, chronological arrival months, total stay nights, booking outcomes, and ordered lead-time bands.

  3. Handle missing values

    Labeled missing countries as Unknown and kept missing children, agent, and company values blank.

  4. Validate the report

    Reconciled booking counts and headline measures with the source and checked that the hotel filter updates the report correctly.

City Hotel accounts for 79,330 bookings, compared with 40,060 for Resort Hotel. Across all records, mean ADR is 101.83 in the source’s unspecified currency units. These are descriptive results; the report does not establish the causes of cancellation.

05 / Measures and interpretation

Keep each metric traceable.

  • Bookings: COUNTROWS(Bookings) counts source records.
  • Cancellation rate: canceled records divided by all bookings in the current filter context.
  • Average ADR: the unweighted mean of the source ADR field across selected bookings.
  • Average stay: mean of weekend nights plus weekday nights.
  • Completed stays: records with reservation status equal to Check-Out.

2015 and 2017 are partial years. Missing values and unusual ADR values are documented rather than silently discarded. No occupancy rate or actual revenue is inferred, and small segments should be interpreted alongside booking counts.
