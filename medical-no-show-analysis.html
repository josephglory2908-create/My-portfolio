Medical appointment no-show analysis

An end-to-end analysis of appointment attendance patterns, including data-quality checks, exploratory analysis, visualizations, and logistic regression.

01 / Business question

Which patient and scheduling factors are associated with missed appointments?

The project evaluates age, gender, appointment lead time, SMS receipt, scholarship participation, hypertension, and disability status to understand how no-show behavior varies across patient groups.

02 / Data preparation

Clean, validate, and engineer analysis-ready features.

The notebook confirmed zero missing values and zero duplicate rows, removed one invalid negative age and five negative waiting-time records, converted attendance into a binary target, parsed dates, and created age and waiting-time categories.

Raw CSV→Quality checks→Cleaned records→Engineered features→Analysis

03 / Key findings

Waiting time showed the clearest operational pattern.

4.65%Same-day no-show rate
24.65%Highest age-group rate: 18–29
33.00%No-show rate for waits of 31+ days

Gender rates were similar: 20.31% for women and 19.97% for men. The analysis found higher observed rates among scholarship participants and patients recorded as receiving SMS messages; these are associations, not proof of causation.

04 / Predictive modeling

Evaluate performance beyond headline accuracy.

The initial logistic model achieved 79.61% accuracy but detected very few missed appointments because the classes were imbalanced. A class-balanced model reduced overall accuracy to 66.35% while increasing no-show recall to 56%, with a ROC-AUC of 0.663.

06 / Business value

Use scheduling insight to focus operational improvement.

The analysis suggests appointment lead time deserves attention when designing reminder strategies and scheduling interventions. It also demonstrates why healthcare models should be evaluated with recall, confusion matrices, and discrimination—not accuracy alone.
